# Interaction principles

These are product constraints to carry into later lesson and UI design.

- **Portrait-first:** Design and test the primary learning flow for a phone held vertically.
- **One-thumb use:** Primary controls should be reachable and operable with one thumb in either hand where practical.
- **Cards, not tiny controls:** Present one short reading or question at a time in large, legible cards with clear hierarchy.
- **Tap is always enough:** Swiping may be offered as a shortcut, but never make a gesture the only way to advance, answer, or go back.
- **Low typing burden:** Prefer tap-to-answer choices and suggested deep-dive prompts. Require free text only when the user chooses to ask or respond in their own words.
- **Clear state changes:** Make reading, answering, feedback, and optional deep-dive states visually distinct without losing the learner’s place.
- **Accessible interaction:** Support keyboard navigation, screen readers, adequate target size, reduced motion, and readable contrast; do not communicate correctness by color alone.
- **No pace pressure by default:** Keep streaks and timers optional or non-punitive; a short lesson should remain easy to resume.

These principles guide the interface, not the lesson syllabus. The lesson structure and progression will be designed separately after the research library is reviewed.
