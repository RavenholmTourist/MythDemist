# Greek mythology research library

This is the initial research index for Greek-mythology learning content. It is a curated starting library, not a complete corpus and not a claim that ancient Greece had one official canon.

## Two shelves

- **Core / evidence library**: ancient literary, religious, historical, and material evidence, together with modern academic interpretation. This is the authority for factual lesson claims.
- **Flavor library**: modern novels, films, games, comics, and theater. These are useful for engagement and comparison, but are never evidence for what an ancient source says.

“Official library” can be used as a product label if useful, but internally the evidence library is called **core** to avoid implying a single canonical version of Greek myth.

## Research and citation rules

1. Cite an ancient work by book, chapter, line, or section wherever possible; include the edition and translation used.
2. Attribute a version to its source. Prefer “In Hesiod’s *Theogony*…” to “The Greek myth says…”.
3. Record disagreement and variation rather than merging versions into a synthetic canon.
4. Separate literary narrative, cult practice, local tradition, visual evidence, and modern interpretation. They can illuminate each other but are not interchangeable.
5. Treat Roman authors such as Ovid as important reception and adaptation sources, not as direct evidence for a single Greek version.
6. Store full text locally only when the specific edition is public domain or openly licensed for the intended use. Preserve its attribution and license notice, and check jurisdiction.
7. For copyrighted books and translations, keep bibliographic records, links, and original notes; do not copy the full text into the corpus without permission.

## Library status

The initial local text corpus contains selected complete English translations from Project Gutenberg editions whose individual records identify them as public domain in the United States. It is a useful reading and retrieval base, not an exhaustive collection or a claim-by-claim reviewed knowledge base. Copyrighted scholarship and modern retellings remain bibliographic references unless a suitable open license or permission is confirmed.
